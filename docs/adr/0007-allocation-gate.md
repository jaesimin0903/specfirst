# ADR 0007: Reading attestations on chain: an allocation gate (`AllocationGate`)

Date: 2026-10-07. Status: accepted for the demo; deployed on Monad testnet at `0xEE966282544FF5e0429e754856197A13b8340C8A` (`SPECFIRST_GATE_10143`) and run end to end there on 2026-10-07 (Evidence, Testnet run). Testnet and anvil only. Revised the same day after review: the owner can exit on any verdict; shares are counted on the vault's balance, not its answers; allocate takes a minShares floor; an attestation of a future block opens exit; each vault call gets a fixed gas stipend (VaultOutOfGas is gone); an optional pinned suite. Revised again after a second review: progress is assets arriving, so a vault that under-reports its balance cannot zero the books; the owner can resync the books to the vault's balance; the stipends are set at deployment, sized for Monad's pricing; the keeper backs off a vault whose exit moves nothing; tests pin the order of the verdicts.

## Problem
A judge or a curator asks: who reads these attestations? The web, the badge and the CLI do; no contract does, and we have no customer to point at. The answer we can show ourselves is a contract whose money follows the registry: it refuses to move money into a vault that is not compliant, and anyone can pull the money out of a vault the moment its latest attestation stops being compliant. On the testnet demo this pairs with the watcher (ADR 0006): the operator switches the DemoVault bug on, the watcher attests the mismatch, and the gate stops new deposits and lets the exit happen with nobody in the loop.

## Decision
`packages/contracts/src/demo/AllocationGate.sol` (MIT), next to the demo contracts of ADR 0006. It reads `CheckAttestationRegistry` (ADR 0004) and changes nothing in it.

    constructor(address registry, uint64 subjectChainId, bytes32 standardId, bytes32 suiteHash, address asset, address owner,
                uint64 maxAttestationAge, uint256 vaultCallGas, uint256 vaultViewGas)
    allocate(vault, assets, minShares)   owner: deposit idle asset into a Compliant vault, at least minShares minted
    exit(vault) returns (bool)           anyone: pull the gate's shares out of a vault that is neither Compliant nor Stale; the owner: on any verdict
    withdraw(to, amount)                 owner: idle asset only
    resync(vault)                        owner: set the books of vault to the vault's balanceOf(gate)
    views: verdictOf(vault) returns (Verdict, attestationId), canExit(vault), positionOf(vault), vaults(), vaultCount(), idle(),
           registry(), subjectChainId(), standardId(), suiteHash(), asset(), owner(), maxAttestationAge(),
           vaultCallGas(), vaultViewGas(), EXIT_GAS_RESERVE(), MAX_EXIT_STIPENDS()
    events: Allocated, Exited, ExitFailed, IdleWithdrawn, Resynced

- `subjectChainId` must be `block.chainid` (`WrongSubjectChain`): the gate deposits into contracts on its own chain, and an attestation of the same address on another chain is about another contract. The CLI passes the chain of `--network`.
- `standardId` is `keccak256("erc4626")`. `suiteHash` zero accepts any suite; a non-zero one pins the suite, as `isCompliantWith` does (CLI `--suite-hash`, default any). `maxAttestationAge` is in seconds, above 0 (CLI default a day; see Freshness). The owner is immutable, as in the registry and the desk.
- `vaultCallGas` and `vaultViewGas` are the exit's stipends (see Gas on Monad); both above 0 and `2 x (vaultCallGas + vaultViewGas)` at most `MAX_EXIT_STIPENDS` (15,000,000), so the longest exit stays below the 2^24 per-transaction gas cap (`BadStipend`). CLI defaults 2,000,000 and 300,000.
- A position per vault: `shares` (what allocations added, then the vault's `balanceOf(gate)` after an exit or a resync), `assetsIn` and `assetsOut` (summed, measured on the asset's balance), and the blocks of the last allocation and the last exit attempt.

### The rules
`verdictOf` reads the registry's latest attestation of `(subjectChainId, vault, standardId)` and applies these in order; the first that holds is the verdict. Stale comes after every rule that opens exit, so an old attestation that is also revoked, failing, from a removed issuer, of other code, of a future block or of another suite opens exit to anyone; a test pins that order.

| Verdict | When | allocate | exit by anyone | exit by the owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NoAttestation | `latest` reverts with the registry's `NoAttestation` | refused | open | open |
| NotCompliant | `isCompliant` is false: Revoked, or any fail, inconclusive or not-applicable result | refused | open | open |
| OtherSuite | the gate pins a suite and the attestation has another | refused | open | open |
| UntrustedIssuer | its issuer is no longer `isIssuer` | refused | open | open |
| CodeMismatch | its `subjectCodeHash` is not `vault.codehash` | refused | open | open |
| FutureBlock | its `blockNumber` is above `block.number` | refused | open | open |
| Stale | issued more than `maxAttestationAge` seconds ago | refused | closed | open |
| Compliant | none of the above | allowed | closed | open |

allocate also needs the vault's `asset()` to be the gate's (`AssetMismatch`), enough idle asset, and the vault's `balanceOf(gate)` to rise by at least one share (`NoSharesMinted`) and by at least `minShares` (`TooFewShares`). Each refusal is a custom error named after the verdict (`NoAttestation`, `NotCompliant`, `OtherSuite`, `UntrustedIssuer`, `CodeMismatch`, `FutureBlock`, `StaleAttestation`).

- Why exit stays closed to others on Stale: the watcher records an unchanged re-check without a transaction (ADR 0006, attestation policy), so an old attestation of a healthy vault is the normal case. Opening exit on age would let anyone pull money out of healthy vaults whenever the watcher is quiet. Age only stops new money.
- Why the owner can always exit: once the watcher stops (after the hackathon, or for a day), every vault turns Stale and nobody else may exit; without this the money would wait for an issuer to revoke. The CLI asks for `--as-owner` before the owner's key exits a Compliant or Stale vault, so a healthy vault is not drained by habit; `keep` exits only what anyone may exit.
- Why a removed issuer and another code hash open exit: the attestation that let the money in is no longer one the gate would accept. The registry keeps calling it compliant (ADR 0004, Roles: removal does not touch past attestations); the gate does not. Removing an issuer therefore opens exit on every vault whose latest attestation it made. Rotating an issuer key is: make the new key an issuer, re-attest each vault with it (a new check, or the same report again at a later block), and only then remove the old key.
- Why a future block opens exit: the vault is on the gate's chain, so an attestation of a block this chain has not reached is not about the vault as it is. It would also keep every honest attestation out (`OlderThanLatest`) until someone revokes it. Once the chain passes that block it is an ordinary attestation again.
- Why the code hash and not the implementation: the vault is on the gate's chain, so `vault.codehash` can be compared with the attested `subjectCodeHash`. For a proxy that hash is the proxy's own code, and an upgrade behind it changes nothing the gate can see; `implementationKey` has no generic on-chain recomputation. DemoVaults are not proxies.
- Only the registry's `NoAttestation` selector means "none". Any other failure of the read, an out-of-gas included, is passed on: a caller who starves the read must not make a compliant vault look unattested.
- minShares: the vault's own quote can be moved before the deposit, the textbook case being a donation into an empty vault that makes 100 tokens buy 1 share. The CLI passes the vault's `previewDeposit` less 1% unless `--min-shares` is given; that guards against a price that moves between the quote and the deposit, not against a vault that misquotes.

### Freshness
The age runs from `issuedAt`, when the attestation was sent. The watcher attests a vault at its first check and at a confirmed change, not at an unchanged sweep, so with the default of a day, allocate into a vault whose result has not changed for a day is refused (Stale) until a new check is attested: a desk request, or `specfirst check <vault>` then `specfirst cert attest <report>` with an issuer key. The CLI refusal says so. A gate meant to run unattended would take a longer age, or an issuer that re-attests on a schedule.

### Exit against a hostile vault
Vault calls are external and untrusted. allocate, exit, withdraw and resync share one reentrancy lock (a storage flag, as in the desk), and exit clears the position before it calls the vault.
- Gas. Each vault call in exit gets exactly a fixed stipend: `vaultCallGas` for redeem and withdraw, `vaultViewGas` for maxWithdraw and balanceOf. Before each call exit requires the stipend times 64/63 (so the 63/64 rule cannot shorten it) plus `EXIT_GAS_RESERVE` (100,000) for the call and the bookkeeping after it, and reverts with `InsufficientGas` otherwise. A caller with too little gas gets a revert, so `eth_estimateGas`, which looks for the lowest gas that does not revert, finds the gas an honest exit needs, however deep the vault's calls go. Whatever a call does with its stipend (revert, run out, answer without doing anything) is the vault's own behavior. With the CLI's stipends and forge's (Ethereum) prices, an honest DemoVault exit needs a gas limit of about 2.19M and one that falls back to withdraw after a redeem that burned its stipend about 4.2M; Monad's cold accesses add a little to both.
- Progress is assets. exit tries `redeem(shares)`; when no assets arrived on the gate's asset balance, whatever redeem answered, it tries `withdraw(maxWithdraw(gate))`. When no assets arrived either, exit emits `ExitFailed` with the first 4 bytes of each revert (zero for a call that answered without paying), keeps the books and returns false instead of reverting: a vault that does nothing and reports a balance of 0 cannot zero the books. A vault's balance going down without paying is not an exit.
- Books after assets arrived. They become the vault's `balanceOf(gate)`, never above what they were, so a vault that overstates what it burned cannot make the gate forget shares it still holds. A vault that pays and then does not answer balanceOf is taken to have taken back what was asked (books 0), so the keeper does not retry it forever; if it kept some, `resync` recovers them once it answers.
- `resync(vault)` (owner, a vault the gate allocated to, `NotAllocated` otherwise) sets the books to the vault's `balanceOf(gate)` and emits `Resynced(vault, fromShares, toShares)`. It reverts when the vault does not answer. It trusts the vault's balance, which is the vault's word as everything about its shares is; it exists for the owner to correct the books after a vault misreported, not for others.
- At most 32 bytes of what a vault returns are copied, so a 600 KB revert costs the vault, not the gate. A vault re-entering exit, allocate, withdraw or resync gets `Reentrancy`; from inside `redeem` that is one more refusal.

### Gas on Monad
Monad prices state access differently from Ethereum and charges a transaction its gas limit, not the gas it used ([opcode pricing](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/opcode-pricing), [gas pricing](https://docs.monad.xyz/developer-essentials/gas-pricing)): a cold account access costs 10,100 instead of 2,600 and a cold storage access 8,100 per 128-slot page instead of 2,100 per slot; warm accesses cost 100 on both. A vault two contracts deep touches several cold accounts and pages on each call, so a stipend that fits on Ethereum can be short on Monad, and an honest vault would then end in `ExitFailed` at every gas limit. The stipends are therefore constructor immutables rather than constants: the CLI deploys with 2,000,000 per call and 300,000 per read (`--vault-call-gas`, `--vault-view-gas`), and a gate for heavier vaults takes larger ones. The price of a larger stipend is the gas limit every exit pays for, since Monad charges the limit. `EXIT_GAS_RESERVE` is 100,000 for the same reason: the bookkeeping after the last call may load a cold page and write a fresh slot.

### The keeper
`watch demo gate keep` (`GateKeeper` in `@specfirst/core`) looks at every vault of the gate each pass, in the gate's order. A vault with no shares is skipped after one read, one whose exit is closed after its verdict. For the rest it simulates exit first (an `eth_call`, no gas). Nothing more is sent to that vault for a minute, doubling after each further such try up to an hour, when the simulation returns false (the vault would move nothing), when a sent exit is mined as `ExitFailed`, when it took the books down by no more than `GATE_DUST_BPS` (0.1%) of them (a vault that pays a wei per exit would otherwise cost a full exit gas limit every pass), or when a sent exit reverted or its receipt failed. Each step is logged once (`watch-demo-gate-backoff`, with the reason), the passes in between say nothing. The wait ends when an exit takes back more than dust, the exit closes or the position empties. A failed read or simulation, or a send that never left, is reported and starts no wait: nothing was paid for, and an RPC hiccup does not hold an exit back. Exit is open to anyone, so the keeper signs with a key of its own (`--key-env`, default `KEEPER_PRIVATE_KEY` when set, else `REQUESTER_PRIVATE_KEY`), never the owner's: a keeper key on a server can then pull money only out of vaults that are not compliant, and only into the gate.

### Reading it without an indexer
The web reads state, not history: `vaults()`, `positionOf`, `verdictOf` and the registry's `latest`, `isCompliant` and `isIssuer` go out as multicalls. Events come from `eth_getLogs` of the blocks each position points at (its last allocation and last exit attempt) plus one 100-block window at the head: a few requests, within the testnet RPC's 15 requests a second and the 100-block `getLogs` limit of some endpoints, never a scan from the deployment.

## What it does not guarantee
- The issuer trust model of ADR 0005 still applies. Any current issuer can attest anything for any subject, and the registry cannot tell a true attestation from a false one. A gate that trusts the registry's issuers trusts each of them; a gate that trusts a single issuer trusts that issuer. A real integrator would pin its own issuers as well as the suite, which this demo does not.
- Compliant means only what `isCompliant` means (ADR 0004): this suite passed at this block for the code the issuer reported. Not an audit, not safety.
- Freshness is measured from `issuedAt`, not from the block that was checked; an issuer can attest an old block late.
- Exit is not instant. Between the bug and the exit lie the watcher's confirmation (a second check at a later block, ADR 0006) and the keeper's poll. Money that the vault loses in that window is lost.
- The gate cannot force a vault: one that refuses or burns its stipends keeps the shares. Its balanceOf is the vault's word too: the gate counts no exit without assets and never counts above what it had, but a vault that pays and then misreports its balance can still leave the books wrong until the owner resyncs them.
- Demo scope: one asset, one standard, an immutable owner, no fees, idle funds minted from DemoToken.

## CLI and web
`specfirst watch demo gate <deploy|allocate|exit|keep|status>` on monad-testnet or anvil (monad-mainnet is refused before a key is read), signed with `DEPLOYER_PRIVATE_KEY` through the guarded wallet; `--network`, `--rpc`, `--registry-attest`, `--once` and `--poll` are the ones of `watch`. `deploy --asset <DemoToken> [--max-age <sec>] [--fund <tokens>] [--suite-hash <bytes32>]` makes the signer the owner and mints `--fund` (default 100) DemoToken to the gate; the gate address comes from `--gate` or `SPECFIRST_GATE_<chainId>`. `allocate <vault> <amount> [--min-shares <shares>]`. `exit <vault> [--as-owner]`: any key may exit what anyone may exit; the owner's key needs `--as-owner` for a Compliant or Stale vault, and also when the verdict or the owner cannot be read (the command refuses rather than guesses). `resync <vault>` (owner). `keep` runs a pass every `--poll` seconds (default 15) as described under The keeper. `deploy` also takes `--vault-call-gas` and `--vault-view-gas`. Failures and refusals exit with 19, like the rest of `watch demo`. The ABI and bytecode are generated into `@specfirst/core/gate` (`packages/core/scripts/gen-gate-artifact.mjs`, browser-safe) like the desk's.

`/watch/gate` (linked from `/watch`) reads the gate named by `NEXT_PUBLIC_SPECFIRST_GATE_10143` per request and every 15 seconds: the gate's settings (suite, freshness) and idle balance, each vault's position, verdict, `isCompliant` and latest attestation, whether anyone can exit it now, and the recent `Allocated`, `Exited`, `ExitFailed` and `IdleWithdrawn` events with who called them. Unset, it says the gate is not deployed.
